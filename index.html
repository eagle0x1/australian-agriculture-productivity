What is Total Factor Productivity (TFP)?

Rather than measuring single yields like tonnes of wheat per hectare, Total Factor Productivity (TFP) compares a farm's total combined outputs (crops, livestock, wool, milk) against its total combined inputs (land, labour, machinery, fuel, fertiliser, and chemicals). When TFP rises, farmers are producing more food and fibre from fewer resources.

Competing Without a Safety Net

According to the OECD Agricultural Policy Monitoring and Evaluation (2025) and USDA International Productivity datasets, Australian farmers receive just 2.4% of their gross receipts from government support—compared to 17.8% across the European Union, 37.6% in Japan, and 44.2% in South Korea.

Exposure to global export competition forced Australian agriculture to innovate early, lifting its cumulative TFP index to 164.2 (1990 = 100).

From Wool to Wheat & Oilseeds

In 1985, Australia still "rode on the sheep's back," with Wool & Sheepmeat ranking #1 in real gross production value. Following the collapse of the Wool Reserve Price Scheme in 1991, land shifted rapidly into winter cropping and oilseeds.

Oilseeds & Canola climbed from #6 to #4 nationally as growers integrated canola into wheat rotations for disease break and export biofuel demand.
